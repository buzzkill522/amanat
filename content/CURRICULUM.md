# The lesson plan

Eleven modules, each written three times — once at each level of difficulty.
The course has one spine: **earn or receive money → keep it somewhere safe →
make it grow → know what you are already owed.** Every module is a step along
that line, and the later ones only work because the earlier ones happened.

## The eleven

| # | Module | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is Money? | The foundation. Money as a swap, and what gives it value. |
| 2 | Saving vs Spending | Why keeping some back beats spending it all. |
| 3 | Needs vs Wants | The judgement that makes saving possible. |
| 4 | Making a Budget | Turning that judgement into a plan. |
| 5 | Why Open a Bank Account | The account everything else plugs into. |
| 6 | Earning Money | Where income comes from. |
| 7 | Interest and Growth | Money growing by itself. Compounding. |
| 8 | Staying Safe | Scams, OTPs, PINs. Placed before the schemes on purpose. |
| 9 | Mutual Funds and SIPs | Growth that is not guaranteed, and the risk that pays for it. |
| 10 | Your UDID Card and What It Unlocks | The gateway document, and the money behind it. |
| 11 | Schemes for School, Skills and Work | Study, training and the reserved share of jobs. |

## Why the order is the order

**Safety (8) comes before schemes (9–11).** A child who has just learned that
money can arrive from a scheme, or grow in a fund, is exactly the child a scam
targets. They meet "never share your OTP" and "a guaranteed return is a warning
sign" *before* they meet the schemes that put money in their hands.

**The bank account (5) comes before everything financial.** Scholarship money,
pension money and fund redemptions are all paid only into an account in the
holder's own name. Module 11 closes the loop by pointing back at module 5.

**Funds (9) come after interest (7).** Compounding has to be understood before
market risk means anything; otherwise "it might grow more" is just a bigger
number with no cost attached.

## The three schemes modules, condensed

Module 10 covers the **money and the card**: UDID as the gateway, benchmark
disability at 40%, ADIP for hearing aids and cochlear implants, state disability
pensions, IGNDPS for BPL households, travel concessions, Ayushman Bharat, and —
at Level 3 — Sections 80U and 80DD, which are among the most-missed entitlements
of all because they need no receipts.

Module 11 covers **study and work**: Pre-Matric and Post-Matric scholarships,
the National Fellowship for M.Phil. and Ph.D., the Free Coaching Scheme,
PM-DAKSH vocational training, the 4% job reservation with 1% held for deaf and
hard-of-hearing candidates alone, NHFDC loans for self-employment, and the state
schemes that vary too much to list.

Module 11's Level 3 is where the course lands: a scholarship treated as spending
money disappears, and treated as savings becomes the start of something. Budget
it, bank it, grow it, and know what you are owed.

## Two rules the content must keep

**Funds are never taught without risk in the same breath.** Every level that
mentions a mutual fund also says the value can fall below what was put in, and
that a guaranteed high return is a warning sign. The brief asked for the
benefits of mutual funds; teaching those alone, to an audience this
over-targeted by people selling things, would be marketing rather than literacy.

**Schemes are framed as offsetting a disadvantage, not granting an advantage.**
Deafness costs money that hearing people never spend. The finding worth
building on is that these entitlements are badly underclaimed — so the lessons
push towards *applying*, and repeat that nothing arrives on its own.

## Before this is taught

Every figure in the schemes modules traces to
[SCHEMES-RESEARCH.md](SCHEMES-RESEARCH.md), which carries its sources and the
date each was checked. **Rates, income limits and deadlines change annually.**

Nothing here has been reviewed by a Deaf adult or by a financial professional.
Both should happen before this goes in front of a class, and the state-specific
schemes need adding by whoever teaches it.
