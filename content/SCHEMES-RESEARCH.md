# Schemes research - what the lessons are built from

Researched August 2026. **Every figure here has a date on it, because most of
them move.** Before teaching a number from this page, check it against the
primary source in the same row. A stale interest rate taught as fact is worse
than no number at all.

Nothing here is financial advice, and nobody who wrote it is a licensed
adviser. It is source material for a school curriculum. Families making
decisions about their own money should talk to someone qualified - ideally
someone who can communicate with them directly in ISL.

---

## 1. The bank account - the strongest thing we can teach

This is the part of the curriculum with the most immediate payoff, because a
child in the site's own age range can act on it.

| Fact | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum age | **10 years** to open a PMJDY account. A minor aged 10–18 holds it, a guardian manages it until 18. | [PMJDY / BSBDA guidance](https://www.bankbazaar.com/saving-schemes/zero-balance-account-through-pm-jan-dhan-yojana.html) |
| Minimum balance | **Zero.** No minimum, no penalty for an empty account. | as above |
| Debit card | RuPay card with **₹1 lakh accidental cover** | as above |
| Life cover | **₹30,000** (conditions apply, check current terms) | as above |
| Account type | BSBDA - Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account | as above |

**Why this matters more for this audience.** A Deaf child who can open and
operate an account at ten is a Deaf adult who does not have to hand their money
to a hearing relative to manage. Financial dependence is one of the routes by
which disabled people lose control of their own earnings.

### The right to be served

- **RPwD Act 2016, Section 46** obliges service providers, banks included, to
  make services and information accessible.
- The **RBI Digital Banking Channels Authorisation Directions, 2025** (in force
  1 January 2026) make the Ministry of Finance accessibility standards
  mandatory.
- Following Supreme Court directions, the **RBI KYC Master Direction** was
  amended so digital KYC accepts thumb-impression images, which removes one
  barrier that had been used to turn disabled applicants away.
- Sources: [RBI accessibility guidance](https://www.eyeway.org.in/?q=rbi-guidelines-persons-disabilities),
  [accessible banking directions summary](https://www.vervali.com/in/blog/rbi-guidelines-for-accessibility-testing),
  [XRCVC, Banking on Accessibility](https://xrcvc.org/docs/Banking%20on%20Accessibility%20by%20XRCVC%20June%202022.pdf)

A bank refusing an account because the applicant is Deaf is not following the
law. Level 3 should teach that plainly, and teach who to complain to.

## 3. Mutual funds - and the honest version of them

A minor **can** hold mutual fund units:

- The minor is the **sole holder**. A guardian only operates the folio; it
  cannot be a joint holding.
- The guardian must be **KYC-compliant**; the minor may use the guardian's PAN.
- A **SIP** runs until the minor turns 18, then stops automatically.
- On the eighteenth birthday the folio **freezes** until it is converted to
  major status - a real trap for families who do not know it is coming.
- Redemption money goes **only to the minor's verified bank account**, which is
  another reason the bank account comes first.
- Sources: [SEBI circular on investment for minors through a guardian](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/may-2023/investment-in-units-of-mutual-funds-in-the-name-of-minor-through-guardian_71148.html),
  [minor SIP mechanics](https://kuvera.in/blog/can-a-minor-invest-in-mutual-funds-through-sips/)

**The part the brief did not ask for but the lesson must carry.** Mutual funds
are market-linked. Returns are not guaranteed, past performance does not
predict future performance, and the value can fall - including below what was
put in. Teaching children the "benefits of mutual funds" without teaching that
is not financial literacy, it is marketing, and this audience is already
over-targeted by people selling things. Every level that mentions funds must
put the risk in the same breath as the growth.

Rule of thumb for the curriculum: **PPF and a bank deposit promise a number.
A mutual fund promises nothing.** That is the whole difference, and a
thirteen-year-old can hold it.

## 4. Disability-specific provisions

Framing note: these exist to **offset a disadvantage**, not to grant an
advantage. Disability costs money - hearing aids, interpreters, transport,
lost earnings from discrimination. These provisions are partial compensation.
Teaching them as a bonus would be both wrong and, to a Deaf teenager who has
met the disadvantage, insulting. The genuine finding worth building on is that
**they are badly underclaimed** - families frequently do not know the UDID
exists, and an entitlement nobody claims is worth nothing.

### The UDID card - the gateway

Almost everything else keys off it. Apply online after obtaining a disability
certificate from a government medical board.
Source: [UDID guidance](https://nayi-disha.org/article/certificates-schemes/udid-for-people-with-disabilities/)

### Income tax, Section 80U (for the disabled person themselves)

| Degree of disability | Deduction |
| --- | --- |
| 40%–79% | **₹75,000** |
| 80% or more (severe) | **₹1,25,000** |

Section 80DD covers a dependant with a disability instead. This is a flat
deduction - it does not require receipts for spending.
Source: [disability tax provisions](https://indiaautismcenter.org/blog/list-of-government-schemes-for-disabled-persons-in-india/)

### Employment reservation, RPwD Act Section 34

4% of vacancies in government posts, split 1% per category group. **Deaf and
hard of hearing hold their own 1%** - not shared with other disabilities.
Sources: [RPwD employment guide](https://blog.ipleaders.in/disability-employment-detailed-guide-rpwd-act-2016/),
[RPwD Act summary](https://wecapable.com/rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-act-2016-india/)

### ⚠️ Correction: decibels are not percentages

An earlier draft of this document said the qualifying threshold for the deaf
category was "60% hearing loss". **That conflated two different numbers and is
wrong.** Checked again August 2026:

- The RPwD Act defines **"deaf"** as 70 dB hearing loss or more in speech
  frequencies in both ears, and **"hard of hearing"** as 60 dB to 70 dB. Those
  are **decibels**, a clinical measurement on the audiogram.
- **"Benchmark disability" is 40%**, and 40% is the figure that actually gates
  the UDID card, the scholarships, ADIP, NHFDC and the job reservation. It is a
  percentage on the disability certificate, calculated from the audiogram by a
  formula - not the same scale as the dB figure at all.

Why this matters enough to flag rather than quietly fix: a family reading "60%"
and seeing 45% on their certificate would conclude they do not qualify for any
of this, and stop. They would be wrong, and the error would have cost them
everything on the list. Source:
[RPwD hearing impairment definitions](https://earguru.in/deaf-jobs/hearing-disability-certificate/)

### ADIP - assistive devices

Long-running scheme (since 1981) covering hearing aids, cochlear implants and
communication devices. Figures confirmed August 2026:

| | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | 40% disability certificate, any age |
| Income up to ₹22,500/month | **Full cost** of the aid met |
| Income ₹22,501–₹30,000/month | **50%** of the cost met |
| Income above ₹30,000/month | Not eligible |
| Repeat claims | Not within **3 years** for the same purpose, from any source |

**Cochlear implants** are the big number and the one with hard age limits:
**₹7 lakh** ceiling for pre-lingual hearing loss aged **1–5**, **₹6 lakh** for
acquired hearing loss aged **5–18**. Covers implant, surgery, mapping, therapy,
travel and pre-implant assessment.

The 3-year rule deserves a line in the lesson: a hearing aid fitted to an
eight-year-old will not fit an eleven-year-old, and the replacement is not
automatic.

Sources: [ADIP, DEPwD](https://depwd.gov.in/en/adip/),
[income slabs](https://divyangkalyan.maharashtra.gov.in/scheme/7-scheme-of-assistance-to-disabled-persons-for-purchase-fitting-of-aids-appliances-adip-scheme/),
[cochlear implant eligibility](https://www.adipcochlearimplant.in/ADIP_PDF/Eligibility%20criteria%20Revised%20scheme%20English%20(4).pdf)

### Scholarships

One scheme, **six components**, which is why they are easy to miss - the
fellowship and the free coaching have no separate name and sit inside the same
notification. All run through the [National Scholarship Portal](https://scholarships.gov.in/).
Figures confirmed August 2026:

| Component | Income limit | Amount | Volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Matric (to Class 10) | ₹2.5 lakh/yr | Fees, book grant, maintenance allowance | ~46,000/yr, 50% for girls |
| Post-Matric (after Class 10) | ₹8 lakh/yr | Fees, book grant, maintenance allowance | ~17,000/yr, 50% for girls |
| Top Class | - | Tuition to **₹2 lakh/yr**, ₹3,000/mo hosteller or ₹1,500/mo day scholar, ₹2,000/mo disability allowance, ₹5,000/yr books | **300/yr**, 50% for girls |
| National Overseas | - | Study abroad | small |
| National Fellowship | - | M.Phil./Ph.D., monthly + contingency | small |
| Free Coaching | - | Coaching fees for UPSC/SSC/banking/state PSC, stipend in some cases | small |

The **₹8 lakh post-matric limit is worth teaching explicitly**: it is high
enough that many families who assume they earn too much actually qualify.

Source: [scholarships for students with disabilities](https://www.india.gov.in/scheme-national-scholarships-persons-disabilities),
[pre-matric](https://www.govtschemes.in/pre-matric-scholarship-students-disabilities),
[post-matric](https://www.govtschemes.in/post-matric-scholarship-students-disabilities),
[top class](https://www.buddy4study.com/article/scholarships-for-top-class-education-for-students-with-disabilities)

### NHFDC - concessional loans (Divyangjan Swavalamban Yojana)

Confirmed August 2026: up to **₹50 lakh** for self-employment, at roughly
**5–9%** a year. A **1% rebate** applies to women with disabilities and to
non-orthopedic disabilities on smaller loans. Eligibility 40%+ and age 18+.

**This is the only borrowed money on the list** and the curriculum should keep
it in a visibly different box from the scholarships, which are never repaid.
Confirm the rate with NHFDC before teaching a figure - the published range is
wide because it varies with loan size.
Source: [NHFDC, DEPwD](https://depwd.gov.in/en/national-handicapped-finance-and-development-corporation/)

### PM-DAKSH - skilling and jobs

Not in the earlier draft and it should have been: DEPwD runs a dedicated
disability portal at **pmdaksh.depwd.gov.in** with two modules, *Divyangjan
Kaushal Vikas* (250+ free training courses) and *Divyangjan Rozgar Setu* (job
matching with partnered employers). **Registration is UDID-based**, which makes
it another argument for the card coming first.
Source: [PM-DAKSH-DEPwD portal](https://depwd.gov.in/en/pm-daksh-depwd-lanuched/)

### Travel - the railway concession, with its catch

Confirmed August 2026. For hearing disability the rule asks **40% or more in
both ears**, and the concession is framed around needing an escort. Roughly
**50%** off sleeper/second/chair car/AC 3-tier and **75%** off first class and
AC 2-tier, reduced on Rajdhani/Shatabdi/Duronto. Applied for as a concession ID
at [divyangjanid.indianrail.gov.in](https://divyangjanid.indianrail.gov.in/).

The escort framing is worth naming in Level 3 as an example of a rule written
around an assumption about disabled travel rather than around what a Deaf
person actually needs.
Source: [Indian Railways concession rules](https://indianrailways.gov.in/railwayboard/view_section.jsp?lang=0&id=0,2,1491)

### Disability pension - and why it mostly will not apply

Confirmed August 2026. **IGNDPS** pays **₹300/month** centrally (₹500 from age
80), for ages 18–79, **80% disability or more**, in a **BPL household**. States
add **₹200–₹1,200** on top, bringing the total to roughly **₹500–₹1,500/month**
depending on the state.

**The 80% threshold is the thing to teach here.** Everything else on this list
gates at 40%. The national pension gates at 80%, so a large share of Deaf
people qualify for the whole rest of the page and *not* for this. Listing it
without that sentence sends families to a counter to be refused, which is worse
than not listing it. The **state** pension is usually the realistic route and
should be asked about by name.

State pensions vary widely - Delhi, Maharashtra, Haryana, Karnataka, Tamil
Nadu, Gujarat, Rajasthan and UP among others each run their own, with different
amounts and their own income and residence rules.
Source: [NSAP](https://nsap.nic.in/),
[IGNDPS detail](https://www.govtschemes.in/indira-gandhi-national-disability-pension-scheme)

### Study and work, beyond the scholarships

- **National Fellowship for Students with Disabilities** - M.Phil. and Ph.D.,
  paying a monthly fellowship plus a contingency grant.
- **Free Coaching Scheme** - coaching fees for UPSC, SSC, banking and state PSC
  exams, with a stipend in some cases.
- **PM-DAKSH** - free vocational training, skill development, placement help
  and entrepreneurship training. The most directly employment-facing of these.
- **Employment concessions** beyond the reservation itself: age relaxation,
  examination fee concessions, and relaxed qualifying criteria where the rules
  allow.

### Travel, health and state top-ups

- **Travel** - concessions on Indian Railways and many state road transport
  services. Rules differ by operator; check the current position locally.
- **Health** - eligible households may fall under **Ayushman Bharat** or a
  state health insurance scheme covering hospitalisation.
- **State welfare** varies enormously and is the biggest single gap in this
  document: marriage assistance, self-employment loans, free laptops or devices,
  housing support, free bus travel and skill incentives all exist somewhere.
  Any school using this should add its own state's list.

### ⚠️ Niramaya - does NOT cover deafness

Worth stating explicitly because it is widely mis-cited. Niramaya is a
₹1 lakh health cover, but it is a **National Trust Act** scheme, and that Act
covers autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and multiple
disabilities. **Hearing impairment alone is not among them.** Do not put
Niramaya in a lesson aimed at Deaf children as something they can claim.
Source: [Niramaya, The National Trust](https://nationaltrust.nic.in/niramaya/)

## 5. What the curriculum should say, by level

| | Level 1 - Start | Level 2 - Build | Level 3 - Stretch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bank account** (5) | A bank keeps money safer than a pocket, and it is free to start | Zero balance, open at 10, what a debit card is | The RPwD right to be served; what to do if refused |
| **Growth** (7) | Money in a bank grows a little by itself | 4% vs 7.1% vs 6% inflation - where the rupee sits matters | Compounding maths; guaranteed vs market-linked |
| **Funds** (9) | Safe places grow slowly and never fall; risky ones can fall | Pooled money, SIPs, and why nothing is promised | Risk/liquidity trade-off, folio freeze at 18, sole holder |
| **UDID** (10) | A free card that says you are Deaf, anywhere in India | Benchmark disability, ADIP, pension, IGNDPS, travel | 80U and 80DD, Ayushman Bharat, the paperwork order |
| **Study & work** (11) | Scholarships are not loans; you never pay them back | Pre/Post-Matric, PM-DAKSH, free coaching, the 1% reservation | NHFDC, state schemes, turning a scholarship into savings |

Numbers in brackets are the module `order` in `content/modules/`.

## 6. Where this is published

Since August 2026 this material has a reader-facing page at **`/schemes`**,
built from `content/schemes.json` rather than from this document. The split is
deliberate:

- **This file** is the research trail - sources, dates, corrections, and the
  reasoning about what to teach at which level.
- **`content/schemes.json`** is what a family reads: one card per scheme, with
  the amount, who qualifies, where to apply, and the catch.

The page sits **outside the lesson unlock sequence**. Modules 10 and 11 still
teach this material in order, and they link out to the page; but a family
needing to know about a funded cochlear implant before a fifth birthday cannot
be asked to finish nine lessons first. If a figure changes, change it in
`schemes.json` and note the source here.

## 7. Open questions

- **State-level schemes vary enormously** and are not covered here. Any school
  using this should add its own state's pension and scholarship rules. This is
  still the largest single gap.
- **Scholarship amounts** are set per year and only the ceilings are recorded
  here; the per-year notification is the authority.
- **ADIP device ceilings** by device type were not confirmed - only the income
  slabs and the cochlear implant figures were. Confirm the hearing-aid ceiling
  before quoting one.
- **The schemes page is English only.** The rest of the UI is bilingual. This
  is the page a Hindi-reading parent is most likely to need, so the translation
  gap matters more here than anywhere else on the site.
- Nothing here has been reviewed by a Deaf adult, a financial professional or
  anyone who administers these schemes. All three reviews should happen before
  this material is taught, and the third would catch things no amount of desk
  research will.
